¡Hola!

You don’t learn words. You learn what you can say with them.

Most vocab books give you lists: all the fruits, all the colours, all the animals. You finish the page knowing twenty nouns and you still can’t say a single sentence.

These books are built differently. You learn about 12 words a night, picked so they work together. That same night you use them to speak. The next night you stack 12 more on top, and every sentence you practise reuses the words underneath.

  1. Useful firstWords come in the order that gets you speaking soonest, not grouped by topic. It can look random. It is not.
  2. Small mixed chunksEach night is a handful of things, an action or two, a describing word and one or two joining words.
  3. Everything stacksEvery practice sentence only uses words from that chapter or earlier ones, so revision is built in.
  4. Joining words drip inThe small words that hold a sentence together arrive a couple at a time, right when you need them.
  5. Out loud, every nightEach chapter ends with you saying it, building your own sentences, and counting the old words you reused.

Questions

Do I need to know any of the language first?

No. Night 1 starts from nothing and ends with you ordering a coffee.

How long does a book take?

One chapter a night, about 30 minutes each. The books have between 79 and 83 chapters, so around 12 weeks if you do one every night.

How do I know how to say the words?

Every word has a SAY IT column written in plain English letters, and each book opens with a short guide to the sounds it uses.

What do I actually get?

A PDF book. Read it on a phone, tablet or laptop, or print it. The exercises have lines to write on.

Which Arabic book should I pick?

There are two: Egyptian dialect and Saudi dialect. Pick the one spoken where you want to use it. Both give each word in English letters and in Arabic script.
